# What makes a useful team handover?

A short reflection on making context, ownership and next steps easier for the next colleague to understand.

## Start with the context

A handover is easier to use when the next person can quickly understand what is happening and why it matters. In this illustrative team example, a short opening paragraph replaced a long chain of forwarded messages.

The note described the current position, identified the next owner and linked the relevant source record. It did not try to reproduce the entire history.

## Three small habits that help

These are knowledge-sharing ideas for discussion, not a replacement for an approved operating procedure.

- Separate completed work from actions still open.
- Name the person responsible for the next action and the agreed timing.
- Point to the current source record and explain any unresolved questions.

## Make room for questions

A handover becomes more useful when the receiving colleague can ask for missing context. Capture clarifications in the shared record so future readers can benefit too.

## Try this with your team

Review a fictional handover together. Ask whether a colleague unfamiliar with the case could identify its status, owner and next action without opening several other messages.
